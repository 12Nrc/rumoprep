What juniors should do before senior year

A month-by-month checklist, from course choices and testing to the summer before applications.

Nickolas CorreaFounder of Rumo Prep · November 2026 · 5 min read

On this page

  1. Junior year at a glance
  2. Fall and winter
  3. Spring
  4. The summer before senior year

Junior year is the last full year of grades colleges see, and the summer after it is when most of the application actually gets written. Students who plan this year spend senior fall finishing their applications instead of starting them. Here's the year, season by season.

The short version
  • Fall and winter: protect grades, take the PSAT/NMSQT and choose between the SAT and ACT
  • Spring: test, pick senior-year classes, build a first college list and ask teachers for recommendations
  • Summer: write the personal statement, finalize the list and decide on early applications

Fall and winter

Grades come first. Junior-year grades are the most recent full year a college sees, and for early applicants they're often the last. At UVA, for example, senior-year grades aren’t considered in Early Decision review unless a student is waitlisted. A strong junior year matters more than any single activity.

Take the PSAT/NMSQT in October. Only the junior-year PSAT/NMSQT counts toward the National Merit Scholarship Program, and it's offered just once a year. Many local schools give it during the school day; Fairfax County, for instance, offers it free to 10th and 11th graders. Scores come back within a few weeks and give a good preview of how the SAT will go.

Choose a test. Almost every college that uses scores accepts the SAT and ACT equally. The ACT is now shorter, and its science section is optional. Have your student take a free practice test of each and stick with whichever feels more natural. Most students do best taking one test two or three times, not both.

  • Senior-year courses: keep the schedule challenging. Colleges see it, and a lighter senior year can raise questions.
  • Testing plan: pick two spring test dates now, with a backup in the summer or fall.
  • Activities: go deeper in one or two things your student genuinely cares about, rather than adding new ones.

Start a running activities list now. The Common App has room for up to ten activities, each with hours per week and a short description. Students who keep a simple document through junior year, noting what they did, how many hours and any roles or results, save themselves a stressful weekend in the fall. Jobs and family responsibilities count too.

Spring

Test. The remaining SAT dates this school year are March 6, May 1 and June 5, 2027. Virginia public schools must now offer juniors an SAT during the school day, in either the fall or the spring testing window (the spring window runs March 1 to April 30). If your student is in AP classes, AP exams run May 3–7 and May 10–14, 2027, so plan SAT dates around them.

Start a college list. Aim for 10 to 15 schools to research, then narrow it over the summer. The DMV makes this easy: there are dozens of very different campuses within a short drive, and a few local visits help a student figure out what they like, such as big or small, city or suburban. Our guide to building a balanced college list walks through how to sort schools.

Ask for recommendations. Many colleges, especially selective ones, ask for one or two teacher letters, usually from junior-year teachers. Ask in person before school ends, while the class is fresh in the teacher's mind. Then follow up with a short note on what your student enjoyed about the class.

Plan the summer. Colleges don't expect an expensive program. A job, a class at a community college, a research or service project, or real time spent on an existing interest all say something about a student. What matters is that the summer has a purpose your student can explain.

A plan made in the spring of junior year turns senior fall into finishing work, not starting it.

The summer before senior year

This is the most important stretch of the whole process. The Common App essay prompts have stayed the same for several years, and the application opens on August 1. Your student can draft the personal statement over the summer and have it ready before school starts. Early deadlines at schools like UVA, Georgetown and the University of Maryland are November 1, which comes quickly once classes begin.

  1. Finish a full draft of the personal statement by the end of August.
  2. Set the final list: about 8 to 12 schools, with a mix of reach, target and likely.
  3. Decide on early applications while there's still time to do them well.
  4. Retest if needed: College Board expects an SAT date on August 28, 2027.

Once the personal statement is drafted, start a simple document listing every college's supplemental essay questions. Many colleges post them over the summer, and some ask several short questions. Knowing the full writing load in August makes it much easier to pace the fall.

Finally, give your student some real downtime before senior year starts. A student who arrives in September rested, with a draft in hand, is in a far better spot than one who spent the summer cramming every possible activity.
